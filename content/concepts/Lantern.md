# Lantern

## Summary
A lantern is an engineered mind (and body), made by scientists on [[Aerth]], that stays awake while a ship crosses [[Etherspace]], where computers fail and humans must be in [[Transitsleep|transitsleep]]. It rides in a small transparent "out-bubble" at the nose of a [[Voidship|voidship]] or [[Voidskipper|voidskipper]], tentacles on a wire-and-pulley control yoke, "a single beacon leading ten thousand human cattle to safety". The name comes from that role. Because etherspace has no time or duration, lanterns see events ahead and behind, learn the [[True Names|true names]] of everything, and sometimes arrive before they set off. Lanterns are recruited from humans with "the mind for the career", who are then physically remade on Aerth. One of their few rituals is to visit their own past selves and make the offer.

## Description (the Sandansk lantern)
- About eight feet tall if standing. Gentle blue skin, scaled like a reptile.
- A small red-rimmed "pucker-hole" mouth that snaps open and shut every few seconds; a voice "like gargled barbed wire".
- Dinner-plate eyes the width of a man's head, with emerald (also "vermillion green") irises.
- One great metal leg, one organic leg, and tentacles. Naked except for a strip of blue silk, with a hyper-geom jewellery pendant.
- Smells of ozone, iron and unknown spices. Cold skin.
- Wealthy: it prepays the narrator's bar tab and wears very expensive jewellery.

## In the book
- [[Ch. 08 — The Lantern]]: The whole story. A lantern recruits [[The Sandansk Station Worker]], who turns out to be its own past self.

## Capabilities and costs
- Operates consciously in etherspace; pilots ships "through impossible geometries".
- Sees past and future events, including "possible futures". Reads true names, and so knows people's deaths to the day.
- Long-lived but "not immortal", so lanterns must keep recruiting.
- The initiation "isn't a pleasant" process but is pitched as "all of eternity in exchange for a little shepherding."
- Will be obsolete in about 3,000 years, when humans learn to bear etherspace "in school as you learned the alphabet."

## Real-world grounding
- The author borrows the premise from China Miéville's *Embassytown*, where only certain people can tolerate hyperspace (see [[Notes on Why Stuff Got Written]]).
- Echoes the Guild Navigators of *Dune* (mutated pilots with prescience). The book does not cite this.
