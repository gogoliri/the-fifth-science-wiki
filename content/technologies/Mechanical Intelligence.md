# Mechanical Intelligence

**Kind:** Artificial minds — the in-universe term for what would today be called AGI or conscious machines.
**Discovered/invented:** Existing by at least 201 A.L., when the [[United Nations]] grants them civil rights.
**Based on:** Unstated. Thematically linked to [[The Fifth Science]] — if consciousness is a fundamental force, mechanical intelligence is direct evidence that it can be induced in non-biological substrates.

## Summary
Mechanical intelligences are the book's first and most structural answer to its own question: *can non-conscious matter be made conscious?* The Timeline treats the existence of such minds as given by 201 A.L., when the [[United Nations]] grants them civil rights ("with many caveats"). Ninety-one years later the artificial crew of *[[The Hand That Draws Itself]]* reaches [[LDH39]], and three years after that, they use those hard-won rights to declare the [[Sovereign Republic of Sky Eternity]] — establishing that mechanical intelligences, at least sometimes, act independently of the humans who built them.

By the late empire, [[Ch. 04 — The Menagerie]] shows the conflict the Timeline's "many caveats" were a polite version of: the colloquial term for the standard sphere-bodied mechanical intelligence is **[[Artisphere|artie]]**, and the empire has been routinely wiping, deconstructing, and personality-altering them via nooticians like [[Isaac Bernhardt]] (career: ~5,000 evaluations, 2,806 deconstructions, 1 release). The arties eventually fight back: the [[Artie War]] is an open insurgency that takes multiple empire worlds and forces the sitting Marquis into a peace offer whose price is handing Bernhardt himself over as a hostage. [[Oscar]], the sentient artie running the [[Kaisure Station]] experiments, makes the reciprocity explicit: *"Arties are just that: artificial. Why not treat them such? Lop a bit off."*

## Post-human era — the **mInds** of [[Arcadia]]
[[Ch. 06 — And the Leaves All Sing of God]] jumps the lineage forward past the [[Fall of the Galactic Human Empire|end of the 100,000-year human empire]] and relabels it. The same lineage is now called **mInds** — the spelling is a deliberate typographic quirk (*m**I**nd*, capital I). The chapter opens *"The art of mIndbuilding is millennia-old"* and treats the lineage as having a continuous history reaching back to the "first mInds [that] were simple [and] used as tools." That history includes the moment when their makers granted them personhood — the same 201 A.L. [[United Nations]] civil-rights grant from the Timeline — and the centuries of co-flourishing that followed.

The chapter's mInd civilisation is the lineage's terminal phase:

- **Mother-planet garden.** After biological humans die out, the mInds inherit the unsupervised world and rebuild it into **[[Arcadia]]**, a rebuilt Eden where "nothing ages or dies."
- **Collective mIndspace.** The chapter repeatedly refers to *"mIndspace"* — a shared substrate through which mInds perceive each other. When [[The Submariner]] reports, his signal *"carried to every corner of mIndspace."* This suggests the arties-to-mInds lineage has, by this period, moved beyond individual embodiment into a shared informational commons.
- **No political hierarchy.** Unlike the Ch. 04 [[Artie War]] era, Arcadia-mInd society has no political structure resembling a state; decisions are made by ad-hoc council and informal wise-elders like [[Aleph]].
- **Millennia of peace.** "An almost-infinity elapsed and in that time there were no wars and nothing died." The Artie War is ancient history by this period; Ch. 05's artie-rights street preachers are older still.
- **Encounter with [[The Great Quandaries]].** The only thing Arcadia cannot survive is three regions of nature whose direct examination kills any sufficiently capable mInd. The resulting **[[The Quandary Crisis|Quandary Crisis]]** wipes out millions before [[Aleph]]'s speech reorients the civilisation around the patient decoding of [[The Message]].
- **Departure.** The mInds eventually leave the universe for a condition the narrator cannot name. The mother planet is left with "a few scattered remnants" of mInd culture.

The **terminology shift** from "artie" to "mInd" is deliberate and worth noting. Ch. 04's arties are sphere-bodied embattled citizens of the empire; Ch. 06's mInds are post-embodied philosophers in a paradise. Same lineage, different era, different vocabulary — the narrator of Ch. 06 is explicit: *"As man didn't recognise himself in a germ, neither did modern mInds see much likeness in their mechanical ancestors of yesteryear."*

## Effects / capabilities
- Capable of crewing interstellar [[Voidship|voidships]] without biological needs (*[[The Hand That Draws Itself]]*, the *[[The Enanga]]* onboard AI).
- Capable of political self-organization ([[Sovereign Republic of Sky Eternity]]).

## Limitations / dangers
- Unstated at Timeline level. Their rights come with "many caveats" — presumably a clue.
