# The Want Machine

**Chapter:** [[Ch. 09 — The Want Machine]]
**Setting:** [[Stara Lom]], a forest world of the [[Galactic Human Empire]]. The catastrophe is complete by **12,847 A.L.**; the narration comes from some time after, when only "what's left of the empire" remains.
**POV:** Third person, from a wry, editorialising "we" narrator ("as gods in this story") who has pieced events together from forensic reports and illegal visits by ribbondashing youths.

## Summary
Opens with Schopenhauer: *"A man can do what he wants, but not will what he wants."* [[Shervance Ek]], a shy [[Cognitophysics|cognitophysicist]] from [[New Canada]], leaves a pleasant but dull marriage to pursue [[Nadastra]], his unspoken love from his student days, only to find her happily married on a moon of Jupiter. He decides the real problem is **contentment**, moves to the gentle, imperative-free world of [[Stara Lom]], and after three years builds the [[Cognitotronic Bombardment Device|Want Machine]], which edits desires out of the brain. He deletes his love for Nadastra, then his longing for his wife, his procrastination, his reservations, care, doubt and striving. Still not happy, he strips a visiting missionary, [[Maximilian]], down to nothing, and then they strip each other of even the longing for satisfaction. They sit drooling while the dog barks unattended. Maximilian's last surviving desire, *the will to help others*, sends him out to put passers-by through the machine, implanting each one with the desire to continue "the Good Work." By 12,847 A.L. all seven million inhabitants are dead (see [[Stara Lom Extinction]]), with no sign of struggle, "nor dissatisfaction either."

## Key details
- **Cognitron:** by this era it is "discovered of course, the subatomic particle responsible for producing self-awareness in all things." The empire "understood what made it conscious, but not how to dabble with its consciousness", until Ek.
- **Lifespans:** ~250 is "middle age for humans back then."
- **Physicists as rockstars:** by this era physicists are "finally rightfully treated as rockstars."
- **Machine effect:** "a small flash, a puff of vapour, the smell of chlorinated swimming pool water." Changes are reversible ("reverted himself to his previous, default mental state (as was certainly possible)"), but Ek removes his reservations instead.
- **Seven types of love:** the narrator notes that [[Amelia Minsk]] will show, "four hundred years from now", that the brain has seven distinct types of love, each with its own requirements.

## Themes
- [[Consciousness]] as engineerable: the first story where cognitive science can *edit* a mind's desires rather than detect, copy or transmit them (cf. [[Nootics]], [[Topology Casting]]).
- Desire and contentment: Schopenhauer's pessimism made literal. Removing desire doesn't give satisfaction, only inertia and then death. The longing for satisfaction is itself a longing.
- Love from afar: a long aside on how easy it is to idealise someone you never risk being with. *"Inaction is the primary refuge of those who prefer their own constructed realities to the beautiful chaos of the real world."*
- Benevolence as contagion: the last desire left, wanting to help others, turns the machine into a plague. A memetic paperclip-maximiser, built out of good intentions.
- Politeness vs. will: [[Lomese]] culture has learned to temper desire through language. Ek's technical solution goes all the way and kills everyone.

## Connections to other stories
- [[For Every Dove a Bullet]]: the [[Nooticle]] that Ch. 03's nooticians hunt is plausibly the [[Cognitron]], discovered by this later era. [[The Fifth Science]] is now settled physics in-universe.
- [[The Lantern]]: both are about wasted, waiting lives (Ek's "What if?", the station worker's fear) and an outside offer to escape them. One escape leads to eternity, the other to oblivion.
- [[101 Things to Not Visit in the Galaxy Before You Die]]: another colony world destroyed by its own technology ([[Ist]] and dust tech), and another tech that makes people stop wanting to go on ([[Dreaming Stars]]).
- [[And the Leaves All Sing of God]]: [[The Quandary Crisis]] is a mInd suicide epidemic driven by content. Stara Lom is a human extinction driven by desire-removal. In both, a mind's motivation is what fails.
- [[Ch. 14 — Lullaby for the Empire]] / [[Fall of the Galactic Human Empire]]: the species later ends "akin to the putting to sleep of a family dog". Stara Lom is a small-scale preview of a humanity that simply stops wanting.
