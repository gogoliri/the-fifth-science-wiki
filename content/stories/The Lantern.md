# The Lantern

**Chapter:** [[Ch. 08 — The Lantern]]
**Setting:** Empire era, unstated A.L. year; a waypoint station orbiting [[Sandansk]], in a bar on one of the station's poorer decks, in the middle of the night (Standard Time)
**POV:** First person, [[The Sandansk Station Worker]] (unnamed)

## Summary
A sleepless, divorced maintenance worker on a waypoint station wanders into a poor-deck bar and finds his drinks already paid for by a [[Lantern]]: an eight-foot, blue-scaled, tentacled, half-mechanical creature with dinner-plate green eyes. The lantern explains that it is one of the minds engineered on [[Aerth]] to stay awake and pilot [[Voidship|voidships]] through [[Etherspace]], where computers fail and humans must be packed into [[Transitsleep|transitsleep]], and that being awake there teaches a pilot the [[True Names|true names]] of things, including their death dates. It names the bartender's and a stranger's deaths ([[Beomus]], [[Paola Hammond]]), then the narrator's own: decompression aboard a [[Voidskipper|voidskipper]] bound for [[Ithaca]], thirty years out. It offers him a way out: ride up front on its voidskipper to [[Absente]] tomorrow at ten, then be remade into a lantern. He politely declines. In the last scene it reveals the truth: *"Everyone refuses at first. Everyone reconsiders a little later. ... We may come to our past selves and make the offer."* The lantern is the narrator's own future self.

## Key details
- **How etherspace crossings work:** complex machinery is switched off and humans are put in transitsleep, or both break. Only very simple life support and very simple piloting stay online. *"The control yoke is linked to the motion fins by wire. ... Travelling to the stars by thread and pulley."* The lantern adds: *"Man will solve all of his problems one day, but starships will always travel by thread. That never changes."*
- **The lantern's bubble:** a small transparent "out-bubble" at the nose of each ship, where the lantern rides awake, *"a single beacon leading ten thousand human cattle to safety."* That is where the name comes from.
- **Time in etherspace:** *"There is no time and no duration in etherspace. All events occur at once."* Lanterns see events ahead and behind, and *"given the complexities of the ribbondash travel process, we occasionally arrive before we set off."*
- **A dated prophecy:** humans will learn to bear etherspace *"in school as you learned the alphabet"*, but not for another **3,000 years**. Until then lanterns are needed.
- **Fixed future, but also "possible futures":** the narrator recalls from school temporal physics that trying to change a seen fate only seals it. The lantern also says lanterns "see possible futures", and offers recruitment as the one branch that escapes the Ithaca death. The ending suggests the choice was already fixed.

## Themes
- [[Radetsky's Law]] / block time: *"If you knew Time's true name you would understand that it is a bread loaf already baked."* The time theory Ch. 05 stated as physics, told here as one person's dread.
- [[Consciousness]]: lanterns are engineered minds built specifically to stay conscious where neither ordinary humans nor computers can. Etherspace is a medium where only a certain *kind* of mind can operate.
- Predestination and self-recruitment: a bootstrap loop. The future lantern recruits its past self, and the refusal the narrator thinks is his own is just part of the ritual.
- Wasted life and fear: the lantern's harshest charge is that the narrator will only discover his true passions as his lungs burst. The offer is less about piloting and more about escaping a small life.

## Connections to other stories
- [[A Dictionary]]: [[Radetsky's Law]]'s "time is irrelevant as far as the universe is concerned" becomes a lived experience for lanterns. The Dictionary's **Retrounification** (sending information along a closed timelike curve) is roughly what a lantern visiting its past self is doing.
- [[For Every Dove a Bullet]]: both stories feature a consciousness that has slipped the usual constraints of an embodied mind (the [[Wanderer]] across bodies, the lantern across time). Both are recruited or spawned from ordinary people.
- [[101 Things to Not Visit in the Galaxy Before You Die]]: [[Ether Orca|ether orcas]] surface ahead of voidships, and the "ether" in their name suggests they are native to [[Etherspace]], the medium lanterns steer through.
- [[Timeline of The 500 Year Climb]]: gives the human cost hidden behind [[Ribbondash]]. The 445 A.L. *[[Geo Milev]]*'s "7 minutes onboard time" now raises the question of who, if anyone, was awake to experience them.
- [[Notes on Why Stuff Got Written]]: the author says the premise is partly borrowed from China Miéville's *Embassytown*, where only certain humans (the "Immer"-tolerant) can bear hyperspace.

## Open questions
- Who on Aerth engineers lanterns, and when does the programme start? Before or after the first [[Ribbondash]] crossings in 431–445 A.L.?
- Is the lantern's "eternity" literal? It says lanterns "live a very long time, but we're not immortal".
- How much of a lantern is still the original human? The narrator is to have "some work done" on Aerth; the lantern has one metal leg and one organic leg, plus tentacles.
- Does the narrator actually go? The story ends before ten o'clock. The lantern's claim and the ritual imply yes.
- If lanterns see "possible futures", is the Ithaca death actually fixed, or only fixed in the branch where he refuses?
- What is the lantern's own true name, and what does it say about when it dies?
- Does the 3,000-year prophecy (humans learning to bear etherspace) come true within the empire's 100,000-year run (see [[Galactic Human Empire]])?
